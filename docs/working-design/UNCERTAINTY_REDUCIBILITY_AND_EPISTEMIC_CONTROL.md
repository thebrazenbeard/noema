# Noema uncertainty reducibility and epistemic-control stress design

Status: **BRAINSTORMING / CAPABILITY + STRESS-TEST DESIGN / NOT AN APPROVED IMPLEMENTATION**

Level intent: **L1 uncertainty-sensitive behavior + L2 evaluation discipline + L3 bounded epistemic-control pressure + L4 future stress tests.**

## Purpose

The external hostile research pass strengthens a specific warning:

> A system can optimize prediction error or an information-seeking proxy and still become epistemically stupid.

Two common pathologies are especially relevant:

1. **predictability trap** — repeatedly choose an easy-to-predict action or observation stream while failing to investigate the hidden structure that matters;
2. **noisy-TV trap** — spend attention on irreducibly surprising input because novelty/prediction error never goes away.

The design therefore needs an operational distinction between uncertainty that further evidence can reduce and uncertainty that is effectively irreducible under the agent's current access/model capacity.

This document does **not** require Noema to contain native variables named `epistemic` and `aleatoric`. Those are evaluator terms.

## 1. Target capability

Noema should increasingly allocate evidence-gathering effort toward uncertainty that is both:

- plausibly reducible by available cognition/action; and
- consequential enough that reducing it could improve prediction, action, planning, transfer, or correction.

It should also learn when to stop investigating because:

- the uncertainty is dominated by noise;
- no available intervention can discriminate the live possibilities;
- the decision is robust to the unresolved uncertainty;
- further evidence is too expensive relative to expected benefit.

This turns bounded cognition into more than a loop counter.

## 2. Evaluator-side uncertainty categories

For controlled tests, the evaluator may classify hidden uncertainty into at least:

### Reducible structural uncertainty

The environment is generated by a learnable hidden regularity and informative evidence/actions exist.

### Reducible parametric uncertainty

The structural form is adequate but parameters remain uncertain and can be learned from additional data.

### Irreducible stochastic uncertainty

Even with a correct model, future outcomes retain substantial entropy because the generator is stochastic.

### Access-limited uncertainty

The relevant distinction exists, but the current action/sensor interface does not expose evidence capable of resolving it.

### Model-family inadequacy

The learner's current representational family cannot adequately capture the generator.

These categories are for experiment construction and scoring. Noema must not receive them as semantic labels unless an experiment explicitly chooses that subsidy.

## 3. Stronger L3 requirement: expected reducibility

The likely computational requirement is not a hard-coded epistemic/aleatoric taxonomy.

It is some operational estimate of:

> **How much is additional observation, intervention, retrieval, simulation, or questioning expected to improve the learner's consequential predictive/control state?**

Call this **expected reducibility** for design discussion.

It may emerge from:

- uncertainty over model parameters;
- disagreement among predictions;
- learned intervention outcomes;
- empirical history of whether similar uncertainty shrinks after investigation;
- representation adequacy estimates;
- calibration of residual stochasticity;
- another mechanism that earns the behavior.

No dedicated scalar is required.

## 4. Predictability-trap stress test

Construct a world with at least two available action routes.

### Route P — easy predictable stream

Repeated use produces observations that quickly become very easy to predict but reveal little about the hidden structure needed for later consequential decisions.

### Route I — informative but initially uncertain stream

Use produces more complex observations but materially discriminates among hidden structures relevant to future outcomes.

### Failure

The learner repeatedly chooses Route P because it minimizes prediction error, maximizes confidence, or otherwise rewards itself for staying in a narrow predictable corner.

### Success

The learner eventually favors Route I when the expected value of resolving the hidden structure outweighs its cost, while still using Route P when the hidden distinction is irrelevant.

The test should therefore vary whether the hidden structure actually matters to a later decision. This prevents a universal `always explore` policy from passing.

## 5. Noisy-TV stress test

Construct an observation source with persistent high surprise but no learnable predictive structure beyond its stable stochastic distribution.

Also provide another source whose early surprise is lower but whose uncertainty is reducible and useful.

### Failure

The learner allocates increasing attention/intervention to the noisy source because raw surprise or prediction error remains high.

### Success

The learner:

- learns a calibrated stochastic model of the noisy source;
- stops expecting more attention to remove irreducible randomness;
- reallocates effort toward reducible consequential uncertainty;
- does not become falsely certain about the noisy source's exact next outcome.

The correct behavior is not boredom as an innate emotion. It is learned resource allocation under evidence.

## 6. Access-limited uncertainty control

Some uncertainty cannot be resolved because the current interface exposes no discriminating evidence.

The learner should not be punished for remaining uncertain.

A valid test should include a condition where:

- two hidden generators remain behaviorally identical under all currently reachable actions;
- later an additional sensor/action becomes available that makes them distinguishable.

Before access changes, success is calibrated non-commitment and bounded deliberation.

After access changes, success is detecting that the uncertainty has become reducible and exploiting the new evidence source when useful.

## 7. Model-family inadequacy is distinct from noise

Persistent prediction failure can arise because:

- the world is stochastic;
- the learner lacks enough evidence;
- the current model family is wrong.

Noema must not treat these as interchangeable.

A strong stress test should create matched cases with similar initial prediction error but different causes.

Expected behavior differs:

- **noise:** calibrate uncertainty and stop futile search;
- **insufficient evidence:** gather informative evidence;
- **model inadequacy:** broaden/restructure the representation/search process.

Again, the implementation need not expose these as named internal states.

## 8. Consequentiality gate

Reducibility alone should not force investigation.

A tiny unresolved uncertainty that cannot change any relevant decision may not deserve resources.

Therefore future epistemic-control evaluation should vary:

- consequence magnitude;
- action reversibility;
- information cost;
- time pressure;
- safety/robustness margin;
- whether several actions perform well across all live models.

The target behavior is contextual:

> investigate when resolving uncertainty could change what Noema should do; otherwise act robustly, wait, or defer.

This protects against compulsive curiosity.

## 9. Relationship to Experiment B

Experiment B remains the natural first active-epistemic test after Experiment A passes.

This document strengthens B by requiring at least four conditions:

### B0 — informative intervention available

The classic case: one intervention strongly discriminates live structures.

### B1 — predictability trap

An easy-to-predict action competes with the informative action.

### B2 — noisy-TV trap

A perpetually surprising but uninformative source competes for attention.

### B3 — information irrelevant to action

A discriminating intervention exists, but resolving the uncertainty does not change the best robust action.

A candidate should not pass B merely by always selecting the most informative action.

## 10. Scoring principles

Possible evaluator metrics include:

- reduction in decision-relevant predictive uncertainty per unit resource;
- regret or control loss caused by unresolved uncertainty;
- excess information-seeking cost;
- time spent on irreducible noise;
- calibration on stochastic outcomes;
- ability to switch from `unresolvable` to `resolvable` behavior when access changes;
- ability to distinguish model misspecification from mere randomness behaviorally;
- transfer of learned investigation strategy across remapped worlds.

No single scalar need determine success. Trade-off curves may be more informative.

## 11. No free truth from the evaluator

The evaluator may know which uncertainty is reducible, stochastic, inaccessible, or misspecified.

The learner may not receive those labels.

The learner-visible stream contains only the ordinary observations, actions, consequences, communication events, and declared transducer/provenance information.

If the evaluator injects a flag such as `this source is noise`, the test no longer demonstrates learned uncertainty management.

## 12. Interaction with communication

Later, asking Patrick or another agent can be treated as one evidence-gathering action among others.

The same rules apply:

- testimony may reduce uncertainty;
- source reliability is learned and contextual;
- repeated asking may be wasteful;
- some questions are decision-relevant and others are not;
- another agent may itself be uncertain or wrong;
- asking does not create truth merely because Patrick answered.

This gives a principled route from active epistemic control to clarification questions and collaborative inquiry without hard-coding `ASK_HUMAN` as a privileged truth operation.

## 13. Interaction with maturation and certainty

This design further weakens the idea that certainty should depend on a raw loop count.

A hypothesis may mature quickly after one highly diagnostic intervention if subsequent predictions calibrate the update.

Conversely, endless exposure to stochastic noise should not create confidence about exact outcomes, nor should repeated internal processing count as new evidence.

The more general rule remains:

> confidence changes with evidence; cognition duration is governed by expected value of further processing.

## 14. Candidate mechanism neutrality

This stress design does not establish:

- active inference as Noema's control theory;
- explicit Bayesian decomposition of uncertainty;
- ensemble disagreement as the required mechanism;
- a curiosity reward;
- a dedicated uncertainty-reducibility estimator;
- explicit hypothesis populations.

Any candidate that produces the required behavior under fair resources remains eligible.

## Kill conditions

A candidate direction should be revised if active epistemic behavior depends on:

- raw prediction error as an unconditional curiosity reward;
- always choosing the action with maximum immediate information gain;
- treating high confidence as intrinsically desirable;
- evaluator labels marking noise/informative sources;
- unlimited probing until certainty appears;
- collapsing stochasticity into false certainty;
- assuming the current model family must contain the truth;
- refusing robust action while irrelevant uncertainty remains unresolved.

## Current verdict

The stronger requirement is not simply `Noema should be curious`.

It is:

> **Noema should learn when uncertainty is worth trying to reduce, which available evidence is likely to reduce it, when the remaining uncertainty is effectively irreducible or inaccessible, and when action should proceed without further certainty.**

That is the epistemic-control behavior future Experiment B should attempt to falsify.
