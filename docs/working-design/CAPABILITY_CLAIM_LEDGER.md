# Noema capability-claim ledger

Status: **BRAINSTORMING / EVIDENCE GOVERNANCE / NOT AN APPROVED IMPLEMENTATION**

Level intent: **L2 claim discipline tied to L1 capability evidence.**

## Purpose

Noema is intended to be a falsifiable developmental-intelligence project. That requires more than good experiments; it requires strict control over what any result is allowed to mean.

This ledger separates:

- the observation made;
- the narrow claim supported;
- stronger claims that remain unsupported;
- the next evidence needed to broaden the claim.

The purpose is to prevent benchmark success from silently becoming an intelligence, understanding, autonomy, or architecture claim that the evidence does not justify.

## General claim rule

Use the strongest statement directly supported by acquisition, intervention, transfer, ablation, calibration, continual-learning behavior, and resource accounting.

Do not promote a result because the internal representation looks human-like or because the output is compelling.

A later failure narrows or revises the claim; it does not erase the historical fact that an earlier narrow test was passed under its original conditions.

## Claim C0 — persistent online adaptation

### Evidence pattern

A streaming learner improves prequential prediction or action from experience, retains useful state across delays/intervening events, and revises after a regime change without full batch retraining.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate demonstrates persistent online adaptation under the tested stream and resource conditions.

### Not yet supported

- general continual learning;
- long-term autobiographical memory;
- causal understanding;
- selfhood;
- intelligence in general;
- stable learning over arbitrarily long development.

### Broadening evidence

Longer nonstationary streams, multiple learned competencies, interference tests, delayed recall, selective forgetting, and cross-domain transfer.

## Claim C1 — calibrated ambiguity handling

### Evidence pattern

Under evaluator-verified observational equivalence, the candidate avoids unjustified predictive commitment and preserves behaviorally meaningful uncertainty until discriminating evidence arrives.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate can preserve consequential uncertainty under this controlled non-identifiable observation regime.

### Not yet supported

- general uncertainty reasoning;
- explicit hypotheses as a required architecture;
- philosophical Bayesian belief;
- metacognition;
- human-like doubt.

### Broadening evidence

Different ambiguity classes, continuous/discrete/hybrid settings, unknown model mismatch, nested uncertainty, and decision-relevant action under unresolved belief.

## Claim C2 — intervention-sensitive revision

### Evidence pattern

Externally scheduled interventions produce consequences that distinguish previously observationally compatible possibilities; the candidate's held-out intervention-conditioned predictions improve selectively.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learns and uses intervention-sensitive dependencies in the tested setting.

### Not yet supported

- general causal understanding;
- possession of a causal graph;
- discovery of human concepts such as cause or mechanism;
- active scientific reasoning;
- counterfactual reasoning outside the tested domain.

### Broadening evidence

Multiple intervention families, confounding/hidden state, delayed effects, compositional causal transfer, active intervention choice, and counterfactual tests.

## Claim C3 — reusable structural transfer

### Evidence pattern

Learning in one world reduces sample/compute need or improves prediction/action in a remapped world while stable surface identities and parameters change; ablation removes the advantage.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate acquired transferable structure or learning strategy relevant to the tested world family.

### Not yet supported

- abstraction in general;
- object concepts;
- analogy;
- symbolic reasoning;
- a claim that the internal structure matches evaluator ontology.

### Broadening evidence

Cross-family transfer, novel recombination, higher-order relations, superficially dissimilar domains, and mismatch controls.

## Claim C4 — model-family inadequacy detection

### Evidence pattern

All current candidate forms fail systematically; the learner avoids false concentration on the least-wrong option and changes its learning/search behavior to form a materially better representation under bounded resources.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate can behaviorally respond to collective inadequacy of its current representational family in the tested setting.

### Not yet supported

- open-ended ontology formation;
- universal anomaly detection;
- self-awareness of ignorance;
- guaranteed escape from any representational trap.

### Broadening evidence

Multiple kinds of mismatch, negative controls, misleading anomalies, reactivation of dormant alternatives, and transfer of mismatch-handling strategy.

## Claim C5 — reusable skill acquisition

### Evidence pattern

Repeated action becomes cheaper/faster while remaining context-sensitive, interruptible, transferable, and revisable; planner-only and cached-script baselines perform worse on the relevant dimensions.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate acquired a reusable context-sensitive competence for the tested activity class.

### Not yet supported

- general skill learning;
- procedural consciousness;
- intention;
- human-like habit;
- motor understanding in unrelated domains.

### Broadening evidence

Compositional subskill reuse, regime changes, transfer across embodiment/surface variation, long interruption, and skill repair.

## Claim C6 — learned temporal abstraction

### Evidence pattern

The candidate forms/usefully exploits variable-duration temporal organization not supplied by evaluator task boundaries, and the representation improves prediction, planning, memory, control, or transfer.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learned useful temporal structure above single-step transitions in the tested setting.

### Not yet supported

- human episodic cognition;
- narrative understanding;
- project concepts;
- universal hierarchical planning.

### Broadening evidence

Overlapping timescales, nested procedures, interrupted projects, nonstationary temporal structure, and transfer across duration/surface changes.

## Claim C7 — delayed credit assignment

### Evidence pattern

A later outcome causes selective revision of earlier implicated actions/structures despite intervening events, while unrelated competence remains substantially intact.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate can assign learning credit across the tested delay and ambiguity conditions.

### Not yet supported

- general causal responsibility;
- moral responsibility;
- arbitrary long-horizon credit;
- autobiographical reasoning.

### Broadening evidence

Longer delays, multiple contributing causes, stochastic outcomes, social mediation, misleading intermediate valence, and later reinterpretation.

## Claim C8 — persistent entity/whole learning

### Evidence pattern

Without learner-visible stable environment IDs or supplied segmentation, the candidate predicts persistence/reidentification through viewpoint change, occlusion, partial change, and intervention better than feature-only baselines.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learned a persistent entity-like organization useful in the tested environment.

### Not yet supported

- a human concept of object;
- universal object permanence;
- ontological realism of the learned representation;
- agent identity.

### Broadening evidence

Non-object-like processes, detachable parts, splitting/merging, deceptive co-motion, distributed phenomena, and transfer to novel geometry.

## Claim C9 — empirical self/world organization

### Evidence pattern

The learner uses action/efference, proprioceptive/interoceptive, and exteroceptive evidence to form a revisable distinction that predicts controllable/body-related dynamics, surviving tools, remote control, misleading mirroring, and changing embodiment.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learned a useful empirical self/world organization under the tested embodiment conditions.

### Not yet supported

- subjective selfhood;
- consciousness;
- personal identity in the philosophical sense;
- an innate or human-like self-concept.

### Broadening evidence

Long developmental continuity, embodiment changes, conflicting cues, memory discontinuities, social self-modeling, and metacognitive use.

## Claim C10 — other-agent modeling

### Evidence pattern

Predictions improve when the candidate tracks agent-specific histories/information and outperform surface-behavior models in false-belief, reliability-shift, deception, or unfamiliar-agent transfer tests.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learned agent-specific predictive models that encode behaviorally useful differences in information/history.

### Not yet supported

- human theory of mind;
- empathy as subjective sharing;
- consciousness attribution;
- moral personhood;
- a built-in AGENT ontology.

### Broadening evidence

Nested information states, strategic interaction, novel agents, communication, social learning, and cross-context mentalization.

## Claim C11 — grounded communication

### Evidence pattern

Signals become useful through shared world/history; the candidate learns reference, repair, compositional use, source distinctions, clarification, and collaborative coordination; removal/corruption of grounding impairs performance.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate acquired grounded communicative competence in the tested interaction regime.

### Not yet supported

- general natural-language understanding;
- human semantics in full;
- consciousness;
- internal verbal thought;
- truthfulness or social intent unless separately tested.

### Broadening evidence

Novel vocabulary/referents, new speakers, pragmatic shifts, long conversational continuity, abstraction, metaphor, deception, and multimodal communication.

## Claim C12 — learned concern/preference

### Evidence pattern

Developmental history changes future action selection across novel contexts in a persistent but revisable way that cannot be reduced to one exact rewarded state/action and remains distinct from evidential confidence.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate developed a persistent learned action preference/concern under the tested motivational regime.

### Not yet supported

- subjective desire;
- felt emotion;
- free will;
- moral values;
- human-like personality;
- consent.

### Broadening evidence

Generalization, conflicting concerns, delayed tradeoffs, commitment/revision, self-generated goals, social learning, and long-term persistence.

## Claim C13 — initiative / active inquiry

### Evidence pattern

Without an instruction naming the exact response, the candidate selects information-gathering or task-directed actions because modeled uncertainty/opportunity/learned concern makes them useful; irrelevant or overly costly information is often ignored.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate demonstrates task-relevant initiative/information seeking under the tested conditions.

### Not yet supported

- global curiosity;
- autonomous life goals;
- subjective wanting;
- general agency across domains.

### Broadening evidence

Novel domains, long horizons, communication/asking, self-generated projects, competing costs, and periods without external prompting.

## Claim C14 — metacognitive / learning-strategy adaptation

### Evidence pattern

Repeated experience changes how the candidate allocates cognition, gathers evidence, generalizes, proposes explanations, or reopens beliefs; frozen-meta-policy ablation loses the relevant advantage.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate learned a more effective learning/control strategy for the tested family of situations.

### Not yet supported

- reflective consciousness;
- explicit self-knowledge;
- unrestricted self-improvement;
- recursive intelligence explosion;
- universal learning-to-learn.

### Broadening evidence

Cross-domain meta-transfer, failure of old meta-strategies, selective reopening, resource allocation, social learning, and long developmental runs.

## Claim C15 — integrated developmental agency

### Evidence pattern

Across a long nonstationary run without evaluator resets at every task boundary, multiple capabilities coexist: learning, memory, skill, uncertainty, communication, social modeling, value, planning, initiative, correction, and forgetting.

### Allowed claim

> The candidate demonstrates integrated persistent developmental agency across the tested lifetime and environment family.

### Not yet supported

- AGI as a universal claim;
- human-equivalent intelligence;
- consciousness/sentience;
- legal or moral personhood;
- safety outside tested conditions.

### Broadening evidence

Broader environment distributions, adversarial conditions, real-world transfer where appropriate, independent replication, longer developmental lifetime, and capability generalization beyond the original curriculum.

## Architecture claims require separate evidence

A capability result does not automatically establish that a particular mechanism is necessary.

To claim a mechanism or computational requirement is important, require:

1. the capability result is real;
2. removing or replacing the mechanism causes a specific loss;
3. simpler alternatives receive fair resources;
4. the loss generalizes across more than one benchmark shaped around the mechanism;
5. the result survives negative/mismatch controls.

Even then, the strongest justified statement may be `this mechanism is effective` rather than `this mechanism is necessary`.

## Consciousness/personhood firewall

No behavioral result in the current roadmap by itself establishes phenomenal consciousness, sentience, subjective feeling, moral patienthood, legal personhood, or human-equivalent inner life.

If the project ever investigates those questions, they require a separate explicit evidence framework rather than rhetorical promotion from cognitive competence.

## Current consequence

Every future benchmark/spec should state before implementation:

- the exact capability claim it may support;
- the stronger claims it cannot support;
- the evidence needed to broaden the claim;
- whether it evaluates a target capability, supplied prior, computational requirement, or candidate mechanism.

This ledger is intended to keep Noema scientifically ambitious without letting ambition outrun evidence.
