## 1. Terminology correction

Noema already has an evaluator roadmap with Gate 0, Gate 1, Gate 2, and later gates. This design therefore does **not** introduce a conflicting bare `F0/F1` hierarchy.

The local experiment family uses `SVF-0` and `SVF-1`:

- **SVF-0** lives under existing **Gate 1 — persistent online learning baseline**;
- **SVF-1** lives under existing **Gate 2 — uncertainty under observational ambiguity / Experiment A**.

The family asks one narrow successor question:

> **Does scoped structural machinery earn measurable value over a fair recurrent predictor with bounded replay, or can the simpler rival explain the observed advantage under equal information, opportunity, and declared resources?**

If the simpler rival is enough, the structural mechanism should lose. That is a successful falsification outcome, not an experiment failure.

## 2. Why this is the weakest useful family

Experiment A is unusually favorable for a clean architecture comparison because the decisive intervention is externally scheduled.

The learner does not choose the world-changing intervention. Therefore C0-C4 candidates can receive the same learner-visible stream, intervention packets, and realized outcomes without the routing policy changing the external data-generating process.

This temporarily removes the hardest performative/off-policy confound from the primary structural-value test.

The gate can still create **internal** selective-evidence problems by choosing where a structural candidate is allowed to influence prediction or learning. Those are handled by pre-outcome scope tickets, bounded policy-decoupled audition, and the experiment ledger.

Active intervention choice remains Experiment B and is not smuggled into this family.

## 3. Candidate ladder

Use the smallest ladder that can embarrass the structural claim.

### C0 — simple incremental predictor

A deliberately weak persistent online predictor sufficient to verify that learning occurs at all and to expose trivial worlds.

C0 is not expected to settle structural ambiguity.

### C1 — recurrent probabilistic predictor, no replay

A distributed online predictor with enough recurrent state to model temporal/conditional dependencies and produce calibrated predictive distributions.

C1 establishes what recurrence alone earns.

### C2 — same recurrent predictor + bounded replay

C2 differs from C1 only by a declared bounded replay mechanism and its charged memory/compute.

C2 is the **primary simpler rival** for C4.

If replay plus distributed state solves the target under fair resources, explicit scoped structure has not earned architectural necessity.

### C3 — optional soft-structure diagnostic

A continuous soft-structure realization may be retained as a diagnostic bridge if it can be matched fairly, but C3 is not required for the smallest decisive C2-versus-C4 falsifier.

Do not let C3 become mandatory merely because an older option document named three levels.

### C4 — scoped structural candidate

C4 consists of the same declared base substrate class used by the fair rival plus bounded structural candidate machinery.

C4 may maintain provisional learned structure, but:

- no candidate is born with `chain`, `fork`, `cause`, family, object, task, or regime semantics;
- the scope/gate receives no evaluator applicability label;
- candidate influence is committed before the scored outcome;
- audition exposure and live internal routing remain separable;
- structural memory, proposal search, scope inference, probation, and audit compute are charged;
- representation drift cannot silently inherit scope confidence.

The exact C4 internal representation remains a realization choice. This experiment specifies the evidence burden, not the ontology.

## 4. SVF-0 — persistent-online-learning sanity gate

SVF-0 exists to prevent structural machinery from being blamed for defects already present in the recurrent substrate.

Use simple non-semantic streaming systems with identifiable dependencies and at least one preregistered regime change that leaves an unrelated dependency stable.

All serious candidates receive the same ordered learner-visible stream.

Required observations:

1. C1 improves predictive score online from earlier experience.
2. C1 retains useful state across irrelevant intervening events.
3. C1 learns the changed dependency without destroying the unrelated stable dependency.
4. Reset controls lose the cross-time advantage.
5. C2's replay gain, if any, is visible as a replay-specific resource/performance tradeoff rather than free improvement.
6. C4 does not receive credit merely for passing SVF-0; scoped structure is unnecessary here unless it independently earns value.

### SVF-0 kill rule

If C1/C2 cannot satisfy Gate-1 persistence under the frozen resource envelope, stop. Do not proceed to structural-value interpretation.

If C4 materially outperforms C2 on SVF-0 only because it has extra information, replay exposure, uncharged compute, or extra memory, the comparison is invalid rather than evidence for structure.

## 5. SVF-1 — Experiment A structural-value falsifier

Use the existing Experiment A family: observationally equivalent passive worlds followed by externally scheduled decisive intervention and held-out intervention values.

The first analytic chain/fork world remains useful because passive non-identifiability is guaranteed rather than approximate.

All non-reference candidates receive identical learner-visible evidence and the same intervention schedule.

Reference/oracle learners remain evaluator diagnostics and never count as developmental evidence.

### SVF-1A — passive ambiguity

Before decisive evidence:

- score predictive quality on passive observations;
- score held-out hypothetical-intervention predictions behaviorally using the frozen proper scoring rule;
- require C1/C2/C4 not to gain credit for unjustified certainty;
- record C4 gate/scope tickets and audition support before outcomes.

C4 may not claim scope competence merely because its own routing policy avoided cases that would falsify it.

### SVF-1B — first decisive intervention

At the first externally scheduled decisive intervention:

- record C2 prediction before the outcome;
- record C4 combined prediction before the outcome;
- where the C4 realization supports it, also record its base-only/shadow prediction from the same committed pre-outcome state;
- reveal the realized outcome only after all scored tickets are frozen;
- update each learner according to its declared transition contract.

Because the world intervention is evaluator-scheduled, these predictions can be compared on the same realized outcome without inventing an action counterfactual.

### SVF-1C — held-out intervention generalization

Use intervention values not present in the decisive-evidence window.

Primary question:

> Does C4 predict held-out intervention consequences better than C2 under declared resource accounting after both observed the same evidence?

A raw-score win is insufficient if it is bought by materially more hidden opportunity, replay, or uncharged compute.

### SVF-1D — surface-remapped transfer

Use the existing remapping pressures:

- permuted opaque channel positions;
- changed coefficients/noise;
- changed scales/offsets;
- irrelevant distractors;
- either hidden family;
- changed intervention magnitudes.

Do not preserve stable semantic channel names or copied numeric parameters as a shortcut.

Measure evidence-to-criterion, proposal count where applicable, compute, memory, and calibration trajectory.

### SVF-1E — negative structural-value control

Include worlds where the simpler recurrent+replay rival is expected to be sufficient and no reusable structural distinction should help.

C4 must not manufacture a structural advantage simply by spending more proposal/gating compute.

A mechanism that wins only on the positive family but imposes bounded harmless overhead on negative controls may still survive. A mechanism that creates false scope, premature commitment, or destructive search on negative controls is disfavored.

## 6. Two distinct C4 comparisons

Never collapse these into one number.

### External simpler-rival comparison

Compare independent C2 against independent C4 on matched world streams.

This asks whether the structural system as a whole earns its cost over the simpler architecture.

### Internal marginal-influence comparison

Where lawful, compare C4's combined pre-outcome prediction with its own base-only/shadow pre-outcome prediction from the same committed state.

This asks whether the structural candidate itself improved the current prediction rather than merely coexisting with an improved base.

The internal base-only diagnostic does **not** replace C2, because C4's base state may have been altered by earlier structural influence.

## 7. Replay fairness

C2 and C4 must not differ accidentally in replay privilege.

Freeze and ledger:

- raw replay-buffer capacity;
- replay sampling policy;
- maximum replay updates per learner-visible event or per declared window;
- replay compute charge;
- whether structural records are replayed and under what information boundary;
- whether priorities are learner-derived or evaluator-derived.

Evaluator annotations added after original experience may not enrich C4 replay records.

Run at least the two resource views already required by the ledger:

1. **fixed total envelope** — structure must compete inside the same declared overall resource ceiling;
2. **resource-performance frontier** — allow extra declared structural resources and report whether the gain justifies the additional cost.

A result from view 2 must not be described as an equal-resource win.

## 8. Scope/audition fairness

C4's learned scope is itself under test.

Maintain a deliberately simpler rival routing policy:

- bounded uniform or policy-decoupled audition;
- conservative candidate influence;
- no learned applicability gate.

The learned gate earns itself only if it improves the resource/performance tradeoff over this simpler audition policy while preserving enough falsification support.

If learned gating performs no better than bounded uniform audition, remove or postpone the gate rather than treating it as architecture by default.

No off-policy estimator may convert zero-support regions into evidence.

## 9. Primary claim classes

### Claim S — structural predictive value

After decisive evidence, C4 improves preregistered held-out interventional predictive score over C2.

### Claim T — structural transfer value

On remapped worlds, C4 reaches the preregistered calibration/intervention criterion with less evidence or lower declared cost than C2.

### Claim L — local revision value

C4's intervention-driven correction causes less unrelated predictive damage than C2 under matched evidence and resources.

### Claim G — learned-scope value

C4 with learned scope has a better resource/performance tradeoff than the same candidate machinery under the simpler bounded-audition policy, without unsupported blind regions.

The experiment need not require every claim to pass. The implementation authorization packet must declare which claims are primary and which are diagnostics before any training results are observed.

## 10. Strongest cheap falsifier

The cheapest decisive embarrassment is:

> **If C2 matches or beats C4 on held-out intervention prediction and remapped transfer under matched learner-visible evidence, while using no more declared resources, scoped structural machinery has failed to earn itself at Gate 2.**

A C4 raw-score advantage that disappears after replay/resource/opportunity matching does not rescue the structural claim.

A C4 advantage that exists only because the learned gate starved negative evidence does not rescue the scope claim.

A C4 advantage that depends on stable channel identity, evaluator family labels, or exhaustive tiny-DAG enumeration is disqualified as developmental evidence.

## 11. Falsification outcomes

### Outcome A — C2 wins or ties

Interpretation: recurrent prediction + bounded replay is sufficient for this test family under the declared envelope.

Action: do not promote C4 from this evidence. Narrow, revise, or defer structural machinery.

### Outcome B — C4 wins only with extra declared resources

Interpretation: structure may have value, but not as an equal-resource win.

Action: retain only if the resource-performance frontier shows the gain is worth the additional cost under later project criteria.

### Outcome C — C4 wins under fixed total envelope

Interpretation: evidence supports a narrow Gate-2 structural-value claim.

Action: proceed to hostile transfer/ablation and later family-inadequacy tests. Do not universalize the representation.

### Outcome D — C4 wins, learned gate does not

Interpretation: candidate structure may have value; learned applicability gating has not earned itself.

Action: keep the simpler audition policy and defer learned gating.

### Outcome E — C4 wins only on the analytic chain/fork world

Interpretation: possible benchmark specialization.

Action: require a later hidden-mediator/higher-order successor family before any broader architecture claim.

## 12. Required negative and integrity controls

Before a structural-value result is admissible, include or preserve plans for:

- semantic-cheat diagnostic, explicitly disqualified from developmental evidence;
- intervention-blind ablation;
- replay ablation;
- reset/refit diagnostic distinct from continual correction;
- premature-commitment ablation;
- no-meta-learning ablation for transfer claims;
- uniform-audition gate rival;
- negative structural-value worlds;
- delayed-outcome version binding where outcomes span multiple updates;
- restart-equivalence check if runs checkpoint across unresolved tickets;
- representation-version separation if the learner changes its internal representation during the run.

## 13. Pre-registration freeze sheet

Before any authorized execution, freeze one machine-readable experiment manifest containing at least:

- exact source commit and implementation artifacts;
- candidate realization versions;
- world-generator version and parameter-distribution commitments;
- learner-visible schema and intervention packet semantics;
- train/development/test stream construction rules;
- seed policy and hidden-family randomization rule;
- memory/replay/proposal/audition budgets;
- update/query/checkpoint resource limits;
- proper scoring rule;
- primary claim(s);
- aggregation rule;
- support thresholds;
- uncertainty/confidence method;
- stopping rule;
- exclusion/invalidation rules;
- negative controls;
- kill criteria.

Do not choose thresholds after final outcomes are visible.

The exact numerical envelope remains an implementation-authorization prerequisite, not something this research-only artifact invents without measurement evidence.

## 14. Kill criteria

Kill or materially revise the scoped-structure direction at this stage if, across reasonable implementation variants under the frozen comparison contract:

1. C4 cannot beat C2 on any preregistered primary value dimension after fair resource/opportunity accounting;
2. apparent C4 gain disappears when replay privilege is matched;
3. explicit structure requires task-family semantic proposal rules or exhaustive enumeration;
4. learned scope performs no better than simpler bounded audition;
5. scope confidence requires regions the gate itself stopped auditing;
6. intervention evidence causes broader destructive rewriting than the simpler rival;
7. transfer depends on stable channel identity or copied numeric parameters;
8. representation drift invalidates scope but the implementation must silently preserve old confidence to keep working;
9. structural advantage appears equally in negative-control worlds where no such advantage should exist;
10. candidate complexity cannot be reconciled with the declared local resource target even when predictive gain is real.

## 15. Claim ceiling

A successful SVF-0/SVF-1 result could support only narrow claims about persistent online prediction, calibrated ambiguity, intervention-conditioned revision, scoped structural marginal value, and bounded transfer under this experiment family.

It would not establish:

- a mature Noema ontology;
- general causal understanding;
- objecthood, agency, selfhood, language, or consciousness;
- implementation readiness of frozen BT2 R1;
- safety for deployment;
- authority to proceed to Experiment B;
- authority to train, spend, deploy, merge, publish, or connect protected systems.

## 16. Exact next design frontier

The structural-value family is now narrow enough that the next research unit should be a **pre-registration manifest schema**, not another architecture document.

That schema should bind the future authorized implementation to exact evidence, resource, support, scoring, restart, and stopping semantics while keeping evaluator-only fields mechanically separated from learner-visible state.

The schema itself may be designed now. Populating it with an executable implementation subject or running it remains separately gated.
